Project overview

stoiXGuard is not positioned as a replacement for stoiXDome and is not merely a sanctions-search utility. It is a separate STOIXLAB project direction for rapid-deployment field intelligence and operational evidence workflows.

The concept is built around teams that arrive at an event, temporary site, controlled area, inspection scenario, or operational checkpoint and need to establish a structured local operational environment quickly.

stoiXGuard can be described as an event-memory and field-verification system: it records what was checked, what was observed, who acted, which media or evidence was attached, what incidents occurred, and what should be reviewed after the operation.

Operational model

Deployment

Rapid local setup

stoiXGuard is intended for temporary operational environments where a team needs to establish a working field-intelligence workspace quickly.

Event memory

Local operational database

Each deployment can maintain its own local event memory containing incidents, checks, observed persons or entities, media, operator actions, and operational notes.

Verification

Structured screening and checks

stoiXGuard supports structured screening, field-data validation, document or identity-related checks, watchlist-style review inputs, and operator-assisted verification.

Evidence

Incident and media records

Operational records may include incident descriptions, observations, attached media, timestamps, operator actions, and evidence links for later review.

Review

Post-event analysis

After an event, the operational memory can support review, reporting, lessons learned, and reuse of relevant historical context for similar future events.

Project boundary

Separate from stoiXDome

stoiXGuard remains a separate STOIXLAB project direction. It may share broader trust, evidence, and protected-data ideas with the STOIXLAB ecosystem, but it is not the same product as stoiXDome.

Use-case direction

stoiXGuard is relevant for scenarios where operational teams need temporary, structured intelligence support: events, inspections, controlled access points, emergency-response contexts, site-security operations, logistics checkpoints, diplomatic or business gatherings, and other environments where field data must become reviewable evidence.

Sanctions or watchlist-style datasets may be one input, but they do not define the whole project. The broader purpose is field intelligence, local operational memory, structured verification, and evidence generation.
